# Methodology: what cell-level control is worth for a Massachusetts C&I battery

MIT Energy & Climate Hack 2026 · REON challenge · updated Nov 7, 2026, 10 pm (v4: 20-year service-horizon economics with replacements, NREL costs and O&M, customers, cell grades, scale-up)

This document describes the full model behind the Cell Bypass Explorer web app (https://claude.ai/artifact/2uKjEAMjq2JzoyPa3ejHJd). It extends Annabelle's Methodology v1 (cell-aging calibration, series-string physics, fault model) with the kW metrics, lifetime optimization, chemistry-agnostic spread, cost of control, Massachusetts economics and environmental value. Every input is labelled **data** (fitted to public data), **derived** (our algebra from stated physics), **sourced** (a published figure, linked in the Sources table), **REON** (stated by REON) or **assumed** (a placeholder we chose).

**Reference results** are from the Python model: 2,000 packs per design for the team default, 600 for the sweeps and 1,000 for the fault cases, in `outputs/value_summary.md` (generated by `scripts/run_value.py`). Energy and life gains are means over paired packs; dollar figures are medians, and the NPV gain is the median of each pack's paired difference. The browser model agrees within 0.5 points and $0.5k (Section 11). All features in this document are in both implementations.

## 0. Summary

**Question.** For a Massachusetts commercial site with solar and a REON battery, how much more energy, service life, firm power and money does per-cell bypass deliver than a conventional fixed series string, after paying for REON's more expensive BMS?

**System.** Two REON EBS blocks (each 30 kW / 60 kWh, sodium-ion; REON slides) = 60 kW / 120 kWh, at an Eversource Greater Boston G-2 site, from 2027. Each block is one string of **250 cells with about 10% (25) as backup cells**, as REON's CTO described it on Nov 7. Both designs use the same cells; the fixed string has no switches, so all 250 run in series and the weakest decides.

**Headline (team default: plain bypass on empty and on fault, no hard cell failures; Python, 2,000 paired packs; money over a 20-year service horizon, Section 8):**

| Metric | Fixed string | Cell-level control | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lifetime energy per block (AC) | — | — | **+12.1%** (mean over packs) |
| Service life per block (to 70% of nameplate energy; mean) | 7.30 y | 8.07 y | **+10.6%** |
| Block-lifetimes used up over 20 years (per site) | 5.5 | 5.0 | −0.5 blocks (32 kWh of batteries not built) |
| 20-year NPV per site (replacements, O&M, salvage included) | $69.5k | $73.8k | **+$4.3k** (P10 +$2.0k, P90 +$6.7k), after REON's +4% BMS cost |
| Simple payback | 2.9 y | 3.0 y | +0.1 y |
| CO₂ avoided over 20 y | — | — | 3.2 t (operation) + 2.5 t (cells not manufactured) |

**With hard cell failures** (same rate for every cell; Section 10), 20-year NPV gain per site:

| Per-cell failure rate | Conventional block repaired (14-day outage, $1,500 visit) | Conventional block retired at its first failure |
|---|---|---|
| 100 ppm per year | +$5.1k; 1.0 repair visits per site vs 0 | +$10.7k; +20.6% energy; 16% of fixed blocks lost early |
| 1,000 ppm per year (Tim's 0.1% example) | +$12.5k; 9.9 visits per site vs 0 | **+$86.6k; +127% energy, +131% life (2.3×)**; the conventional site buys 6.6 blocks per position over 20 years against 3.0 |

**By customer** (same battery; 20-year NPV gain per site): neighborhood grocer on Eversource G-1's demand option (2 blocks) **+$4.7k**; mid-size business on G-2 (2 blocks) +$4.3k; cold-storage warehouse on G-3 (10 blocks) **+$20.7k**; National Grid G-2 site +$3.5k.

**Scale-up:** per 100 MW of C&I storage (about 1,670 two-block sites), +$7.2M NPV, +19.4 GWh delivered, 9.5 kt CO₂ avoided and 368 t of cells not manufactured over 20 years. For context, Massachusetts had about 222 MW enrolled in ConnectedSolutions in 2025 [S28].

For slides: about **+12% energy and +11% life per battery, +$4k to +$5k per two-block site over 20 years (about $2k per block)**, rising to **2–3× energy and life, and tens of thousands of dollars, when single-cell failures end conventional blocks** (REON's own comparison).

**Six findings:**
1. **REON's string design (25 backup cells in 250) gives +12% energy and +11% life per battery**, against +6% for our earlier guess of 4 backups in 96 cells. Over 20 years that is +$4.3k per two-block site after paying REON's +4% BMS cost: positive but modest at the paper's cell spread.
2. **Single-point failure is where 2–3× comes from.** A conventional 250-cell string at 0.1% failures per cell-year has a 22% chance a year of a cell failure stopping it (92% over 10 years); REON's 25 backups make that about zero. If the owner replaces a block at its first failure, REON delivers 2.3× the energy and saves +$87k per site over 20 years. If the owner repairs instead, REON avoids about 10 service visits per site. Per-cell failure rates have no public field number; we show 10, 100 and 1,000 ppm.
3. **Value scales with cell-to-cell spread and with backups.** Spread 2× gives +25% energy (+$15.7k), 3× +41% (+$30.9k); 5% early-knee cells +19% (+$8.1k). At half the paper's spread the 20-year gain is about zero. Backups: 5 cells +$0.4k, 12 +$2.4k, 25 +$4.3k, 50 +$6.4k, every cell +$11.6k.
4. **Cell grades.** REON built with cells 1.5× as uneven still beats a conventional battery with matched cells (+6% energy, +$0.6k); at 2× the energy is equal and cheaper cells must cover REON's BMS cost (a cell discount of roughly a third, since cells are about 10–12% of installed cost [S26]).
5. **Switch resistance is the main technical risk.** 0.5 mΩ per switch (1.6% loss) cuts the 20-year gain to +$2.2k; 1 mΩ (3.2%) turns it negative (−$1.0k).
6. **Resting adds nothing measurable once there are 25 backups**, so the headline is plain bypass, matching REON's framing that it improves system life, not cell life.

## 2. Data and calibration

All calibrated numbers come from the public source data of Zhou et al., *Nature Energy* (2026), repository Rico-dicp/Ev-battery-cell-to-cell-inconsistency (MIT licence) [S1, S2]. The cells are NMC prismatic cells in passenger cars, 95 in series; capacities are estimated from field charging data, not lab-measured.

**Verified from the paper text** [S1]:
- 116 cars and 17 buses over 3+ years, up to 300,000 km. Cell inconsistency costs cars 6.2% of pack health, 17.7% of pack life and 12.9% of power, with βenergy 0.807. Buses (LFP) lose 7.5%, 22.8% and 15.1%, with βenergy 0.73.
- "Normalised capacity" is relative to the **nominal capacity of new cells** (capacity-defined SOH). Reference condition: 27 °C, 50% SOC.
- βlife uses an end of life of **0.85** SOH, because the vehicles had not reached 0.80.
- SOC imbalance reduces usable charge by under 2%. Cars with passive balancing kept cell-voltage spread under 100 mV; buses without balancing reached 300 mV.

**Files used** (Annabelle's calibration, Methodology v1):

| File | Content | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Fig. 3g | Capacity of 95 cells in one car, 74k–303k km | Per-cell hinge fits: pre-knee rate, knee, post-knee slope |
| Fig. 3h | Resistance of the same cells | Resistance spread (CV 0.059) and growth with fade (7.48 per unit) |
| Fig. 4 (a–l) | 95 cells × 12 cars at 80k and 280k km | Knee spread between packs; validation |
| Fig. 5a/5b | σSOH and βSOH vs mileage, 19 cars | Held-out validation |
| Fig. 5c | βlife per car | One fitted target |
| `07_indicator_calculation.py` | Paper's metric code | Exact βlife definition |

**Hinge fit per cell** (form chosen to match the data):

```latex
Q_i(x) = a_i - e_i\,(x - x_0) - s_i\,(x - \tau_i)_+ , \qquad (u)_+ = \max(u, 0)
```

**Fitted structure** (data): mean pre-knee rate 0.0106 per 100k km; mean knee 240k km; post-knee slope 8.38× pre-knee; cell-to-cell CVs of rate, knee and post-knee slope 0.38, 0.044, 0.35; correlations −0.47 (rate–knee), +0.45 (rate–slope), −0.60 (knee–slope); beginning-of-life capacity CV 0.47%; knee timing between packs log-sd 0.139; post-knee spread shrink λ = 0.537, fitted so that mean βlife matches the 19 cars (0.823).

**Independent check** (Isabell, Fig. 4 at 280k km): the weakest cell sits on average 2.92 SDs below its pack mean, and mean βSOH is 0.920. The calibrated model gives 2.77 SDs and 0.934.

## 3. Cell aging in stationary service

The EV data fixes how cells differ from each other, not how fast a stationary block ages. Each cell's capacity at age a (years):

```latex
Q_i(a) = q_{0,i} - m_i\big[k_\text{cal}\sqrt{a} + k_\text{cyc}\,\text{EFC}_i(a)\big] - \kappa_i\,\bar b\,\big(c_i(a) - \tau_i\big)_+
```

- **Base fade** (assumed): calendar fade grows with √time and cycle fade with equivalent full cycles; k_cal = 0.02 per √year, k_cyc = 1 × 10⁻⁵ per cycle, 365 cycles a year. At the knee, 68% of fade is calendar.
- **Cell multipliers** m, τ_rel, κ (data): drawn jointly normal with the fitted covariance; temperature carries its Arrhenius share of m's spread (Eₐ = 50 kJ/mol, 0.068 per °C at 25 °C, cell temperature spread 2 °C, assumed).
- **Knee** (assumed transfer rule): placed where the mean cell has lost 7.5% of nominal capacity, as in the EV data. With the base fade above this is 6.6 years.
- **Use clock** (derived): each cell's knee clock advances with its own use, `c_i += Δt (w_cal + w_cyc u_i)`, so a rested or bypassed cell ages on calendar time only.
- **Resistance** (data): R/R₀ = 1 + 7.48 × fractional fade.

**Chemistry-agnostic spread** (new). One multiplier s scales every within-pack spread: the CVs of rate, knee and post-knee slope, starting-capacity spread and resistance spread (equivalently, the rate/knee/slope covariance by s²). s = 1 reproduces the calibrated cells. REON's CTO says the BMS/EMS is chemistry-agnostic and REON wants to test mixed use; s stands in for chemistry, binning quality, hot spots or mixed-age packs.

**Early-knee outliers** (new, paper-aligned, default off). The paper attributes accelerated aging to cells with "less favourable initial conditions or harsher local operating histories". A share f of cells (default 0) gets its knee multiplied by 0.8. The data's weakest cell (2.92–3.05 SDs below mean) sits slightly further out than the model's (2.77), so a small share is consistent with the data.

**Why the percentages are trusted but not the years.** Moving the knee from 6.6 to 10 or 14 years changes fixed-string life from 7.4 to 10.5 to 14.2 years, but the gain at k = 4 of 96 stays at about +6% (and at 25 of 250, +12.1% with the default knee against +11.8% with a 10-year knee) energy (Methodology v1). The gain comes from the spread structure, which is data; the years come from the timeline, which is assumed.

## 4. Series-string physics and bypass (capacity recovery)

Series cells share one current. Sort the active cells' capacities Q₍₁₎ ≤ Q₍₂₎ ≤ …. If h more cells may drop out during a discharge, the string stops when the (h+1)-th weakest empties, and each cell passes the smaller of its own charge and that level (derived):

```latex
Q_\text{string}(h) = \sum_{i \in \text{active}} \min\!\big(Q_i,\; Q_{(h+1)}\big)
```

- h = 0 is the fixed string (n × min Q); h = n − 1 is every cell running to its own end (Σ Q).
- Check against the brief: 15 cells at 85 Ah and one at 70 Ah give 16 × 70 = 1,120 Ah fixed and 15 × 85 + 70 = 1,345 Ah with h = 1 (+20%). Tested in code.
- **Backup cells k** (REON: about 10% of the string; default 25 of 250): the voltage window allows at most k cells out of the string at once. As we read REON ("the discount is at the power cell, not the energy cell"), rotation lets every cell give its energy; only the number in the string at a moment, and so voltage and power, is reduced. h = k − (cells isolated) − (cells resting this month).
- **Switch loss** (assumed 99.5% efficiency, ask REON) applies to the cell-level design only.
- Energy per discharge = usable charge × cell Ah × 3.1 V (constant voltage; voltage sag ignored).

## 5. Monitoring and control

**Measurement** (brief's given capability). The controller sees each cell's voltage, temperature, SOC, SOH, capacity, resistance, OCV and history, each with Gaussian noise (placeholders: 2 mV, 0.5 °C, 1%, 1%, 1%, 2%, 2 mV, 6-month smoothing). Signals are fused into one capacity estimate per cell by inverse-variance weighting (derived). Decisions use measured values only; aging uses true values.

**Policy, each month, in order:**
1. **Isolate** (optional, off by default): take a cell out for good if it is a set distance below the pack mean, hotter, or higher-resistance.
2. **Rest by remaining life** (optional, off in the v4 headline because it adds nothing measurable with 25 backups): before the knee, rest the cells that would reach retirement first.
3. **Rest hot cells** (optional, off by default; REON: "our BMS can let a 'hot' cell be bypassed and take a rest").
4. **Discharge** with the remaining headroom (Section 4).
5. **Age** every cell by what it actually did.

One headroom slot is kept free (`spare = 1`), and resting and isolation never use it.

**Remaining-life estimate** (new, measured values only). For each cell keep the last 13 monthly capacity estimates.

```latex
r_i^\text{own} = -\text{OLS slope of } \hat Q_i \text{ over the last 12 months (needs} \ge 6\text{)} \qquad
r_i^\text{prior} = \bar r_\text{pack}\,\frac{1-\hat Q_i}{\overline{1-\hat Q}} \qquad
r_i = \tfrac12 r_i^\text{own} + \tfrac12 r_i^\text{prior}
```

```latex
L_i = \frac{\hat Q_i - Q_\text{retire}}{r_i} \;\text{years}, \qquad \text{rest } i \text{ if } L_i < (1 - x)\,\text{median}(L)
```

Rest the lowest-L candidates, at most `min(rest_count, k − out − spare)`, and only while the measured pack mean is above `rest_stop` (default 85%). The fade-proportional prior steadies the noisy 12-month slope; it works because fast pre-knee faders knee earlier (fitted correlation −0.47).

**What the resting results show** (Section 13):
- Resting **before the knee** helps; resting all life long loses energy, because late in life the headroom is worth more for bypass on empty.
- The binding settings are the **count** and the **stop level**. The threshold x does not bind below about 30%, because more than two cells always qualify. The web app's resting chart therefore shows flat bars from 0 to 30%; a sweep over the stop level is more informative.
- **Temperature-triggered resting hurts in this model** (+3.4% energy instead of +7.2%). Rack temperatures are static here, so a hot cell stays hot and is rested every month, using headroom for good. Real hot spots move with load and season; modelling them needs time-varying temperatures (not yet built).

## 6. Output metrics

| Metric | Definition | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Retention | Energy per discharge ÷ nameplate (default), or ÷ the same design's beginning-of-life energy (the brief's R_sys) | Toggle; changes the gain by under 0.1 point |
| Service life | First month retention < 70%; gains are means over packs (a median moves a whole month at a time) | 70% from the brief (stationary); 80% as sensitivity |
| Lifetime energy | Σ energy × availability × cycles over service life | Reported DC (simulator) and AC (economics) |
| Firm kW (d hours) | min(inverter rating, usable kWh ÷ d) × availability; 0 after retirement | What ConnectedSolutions pays for |
| βpower | Pack peak power ÷ Σ cell peak powers, cells at 50% SOC, 2.5 V floor | Paper eq. 12–16, simplified; the absolute resistance scale cancels; bypass may drop up to h of the highest-resistance cells |
| βSOH | min ÷ mean cell capacity among active cells | Paper metric, shown under each ring |
| βlife | First cell to 0.85 ÷ mean cell life | Used in calibration |

βpower at beginning of life comes out at 0.892, close to the paper's fleet average of 0.871 (cars) and 0.849 (buses), which is a useful cross-check.

## 7. Cost of cell-level control and break-even

REON's CTO on the hack Discord: REON's BMS costs "roughly three times the conventional BMS (depends on several factors: cell size, mcu, mos cost, etc.)", and a "BMS consist[s] only 2-3 % of system cost" [REON]. "MOS" refers to the MOSFET switches, so the switches are included.

```latex
\text{overhead} = (3 - 1) \times (2\text{–}3\%) = 4\text{–}6\% \text{ of system cost} \quad \text{(derived)}
```

This assumes REON's BMS replaces the conventional one. Default 4%; show 4–6%.

**Break-even.** Cell-level control lowers installed cost per delivered MWh when the lifetime-energy gain exceeds the overhead, to first order. Every gain chart in the app draws this as a red line. It ignores firm-kW, outage and life value, so it is conservative.

## 8. Massachusetts C&I economics

**Site.** 2 × REON EBS (60 kW / 120 kWh), Eversource Greater Boston rate G-2, first year 2027, solar on site. Two blocks clear the ≥50 kW size an aggregator lists for C&I ConnectedSolutions; the official program materials state no minimum [S3, S4].

For each simulated pack and each year y of its service life, let E be the mean usable energy per discharge at the AC meter (× inverter efficiency 0.96, assumed) and A the availability. Per block:

```latex
\text{CS}_y = \$200/\text{kW} \times \min\!\big(30\,\text{kW},\; E/3\,\text{h}\big) \times A
```
ConnectedSolutions Daily Dispatch pays $200 per kW-summer on average kW held over 30–60 weekday events of 2–3 h, 2–7 pm, June–September, with a 5-year rate lock [S3]. We use 3-hour events (conservative).

```latex
\text{DC}_y = 12 \times \$33.95/\text{kW-month} \times 0.75 \times \min\!\big(30\,\text{kW},\; E/2\,\text{h}\big) \times A
```
Eversource Greater Boston G-2: $20.21 distribution + $13.74 transmission per kW-month (Eversource's 2026 Summary of Eastern Massachusetts Electric Rates, effective Sep 1, 2026 [S22]; small businesses on G-1's demand option pay $41.65 and large ones on G-3 $30.05), billed on the monthly 15-minute peak [S7]. The 2-hour peak duration and 50% realization (75% as a sensitivity) are assumed; replace them with a ComStock load profile [S8].

```latex
\text{CPS}_y = 0.87 \times \text{ACP}_y \times 1.0 \times \frac{E}{1000} \times (365 - 45) \times A, \qquad \text{ACP} = \$65 \;(\le 2032),\; \$45 \;(> 2032)
```
Clean Peak: ACP $65/MWh for 2026–2032 and $45 after [S9]; certificates have cleared at about 87% of ACP and merchant storage earns 1.0–2.0× multipliers [S10]. We use 1.0× and exclude ConnectedSolutions event days, because stacking in the same hour is not confirmed.

```latex
\text{Loss}_y = \text{MWh discharged} \times \left(\frac{1}{0.88} - 1\right) \times \$0.20/\text{kWh}
```
Round-trip efficiency 88% is assumed; energy price from National Grid commercial Basic Service, about 21.7 ¢/kWh (Jan 2026) [S11].

```latex
\text{Capex} = \$600/\text{kWh} \times 60\,\text{kWh} \times (1 + \text{overhead}_\text{cell}) \times (1 - 0.30)
```
Installed cost $600/kWh is assumed for this 2-hour system (installer data for small Massachusetts C&I: $500–800/kWh installed [S12]; NREL ATB's bottom-up commercial 4-hour system was about $480/kWh in 2020 dollars, and shorter systems cost more per kWh [S25]). 30% investment tax credit: storage keeps it for projects beginning construction by 2033, subject to FEOC sourcing rules [S13, S14]; REON's cell origin is unknown.

**20-year service horizon (headline).** The owner needs storage for 20 years. When a block retires (capacity below 70% of nameplate or, in the 'retire' case, a hard fault), the owner buys a new block at that year's installed price, which falls along NREL's 2025 mid-case projection for 4-hour batteries (relative to 2024: 0.84 in 2030, 0.73 in 2035, 0.66 in 2040, 0.53 in 2050) [S23], and receives that year's ITC (30% for construction starting through 2033, 22.5% in 2034, 15% in 2035, none after) [S24]. Replacement block j of pack p behaves like simulated pack (p + 7919 j) mod P, so replacements are fresh, independent draws and both designs use the same mapping (paired). Fixed O&M is 2.5% of installed cost a year for both designs, NREL ATB's value for commercial battery storage [S25]. The block in service at year 20 is credited straight-line salvage. NPV = − Σ capex (discounted to purchase month) + Σ_y (CS + DC + CPS − losses − visits − O&M) / 1.08^(y+1) + salvage. NPV gains are means of each pack's paired difference, with P10 and P90. The earlier comparison over each design's own first life (no replacement, no O&M) is still reported as "own-life NPV". A hand-calculated test checks the horizon NPV (`tests/test_v3_faults.py`).

**Realism.** Demand-charge capture is set to a conservative 50% of the theoretical saving (75% as a sensitivity), because the battery cannot cover every monthly peak. Still not included: interconnection and permitting, aggregator or software fees, and a ConnectedSolutions rate change after its 5-year lock. Massachusetts is among the best US markets for behind-the-meter storage (G-2 demand charges of $33.95/kW and ConnectedSolutions at $200/kW), so paybacks of about 3 years before those costs are plausible but optimistic; the REON-vs-conventional gain is much less sensitive to them than the absolute values.

**Who sells this.** REON's PitchBook profile (Sep 2026) describes it as a C&I energy platform: storage systems, microgrids, energy-management software, DER aggregation, optimisation and dispatch for virtual power plants, and financing [S21]. So REON can plausibly act as the ConnectedSolutions aggregator and finance the system itself. Confirm with REON which of these it offers in Massachusetts today.

**Year-1 value per site** (fixed string): ConnectedSolutions $7.4k, demand charges $17.0k, Clean Peak $2.0k, losses −$1.1k; net $25.4k. Cell-level control is about 1% lower in year 1 (switch loss) and gains later through slower capacity loss and longer life.

## 9. Environmental value

- **Operational CO₂**: extra AC MWh delivered over life × 597 lb CO₂/MWh, the ISO-NE 2024 average emission rate [S15]. This assumes the battery charges from on-site solar and that delivered energy displaces average grid generation. Peak-hour marginal units emit more, so this is conservative.
- **Cells not manufactured**: over a 20-year storage need, an owner buys 20 / life blocks of capacity. Avoided kWh = kWh × 20 × (1/life_fixed − 1/life_cell). Embodied CO₂ = avoided kWh × 80 kg CO₂e/kWh (Fraunhofer 2024 comparative LCA: sodium-ion 75–87, LFP 58–92 kg CO₂e per kWh of cell [S16]); replace with REON's lithium LCA when shared. Material = avoided kWh × 7 kg/kWh (assumed cell mass; ask REON).

## 10. Hard cell failures (single point of failure)

The paper's data has no sudden failures, so the headline runs without them. REON's CTO stressed the other side: in a conventional series string one failed cell stops all of them, so reliability falls as (1 − r)^N. v3 models this directly.

- **Rate**: the same failure rate r for every cell (homogeneous; Annabelle's risk-factor model stays available as an option). No public per-cell field rate exists, so we run r = 10, 100 and 1,000 ppm per cell-year (`docs/Reliability_and_Customers_Research.md`, Section 2): modern EV packs imply roughly 10–20 ppm per series element or less; patents use 100 ppm and 0.1% as illustrations; Tim's example was 0.1%.
- **Fixed string**: any failure stops the block. Two owner responses bracket reality: **repair** (14-day outage, $1,500 visit, failed cell swapped) or **retire** the block at that point.
- **Cell-level control**: the failed cell is switched out and its backup carries on; a visit (or retirement) only when more than 25 cells are out.
- **Economics**: visit cost is subtracted in the year it happens; outage days reduce availability. Outage cost to the business is reported as context, not added to NPV (Massachusetts averages 0.9 grid interruptions and about 2 hours a year, EIA 2025).

## 11. Monte Carlo procedure and implementation

- **Packs**: 250 cells in series per 60 kWh block (77.4 Ah, 3.1 V), 25 backups; monthly steps over 20 years; one representative discharge per month at 365 cycles a year.
- **Common random numbers**: pack p gets identical initial cells in both designs, so differences come from the design, not luck.
- **Reported values**: energy and life gains are ratios of means over paired packs. Service life is counted in whole months, so a ratio of medians jumps about 1 point whenever the median pack crosses a month; means halve the noise (at 48 packs, ±0.5 instead of ±1.0 points). Dollar figures are medians; the NPV gain is the median of each pack's paired difference, with P10 and P90.
- **Pack counts**: the team default uses 2,000 packs per design, the sweeps 600 and the fault cases 1,000.
- **Two implementations**: Python (`reon_model/`, 57 tests; `scripts/run_value.py`) is the reference; the browser port (web app) is the dashboard. They share every formula; the economics code is line-for-line identical. `node webapp/checks/crosscheck.js` compares them on the team default:

| Team default (v3) | Browser (1,000 packs) | Python (2,000 packs) |
|---|---|---|
| Lifetime energy gain | +12.3% | +12.1% |
| Service life gain | +10.7% | +10.6% |
| 20-year NPV gain per site | +$4.37k | +$4.34k |
| 1,000 ppm failures, base block retired: energy gain | +135% (200 packs) | +127% (1,000 packs) |

  The remaining difference is Monte Carlo noise. In v2 the two versions also differed on resting (Python ranks cells from the raw 1%-noise capacity reading; the browser fuses all signals); resting is now off by default, so this no longer affects the headline.

## 15. Limitations

1. **One car sets the within-pack structure.** The 4.4% knee-timing spread, which limits small-k bypass, comes from 95 cells of one car. Packs that knee less in unison make bypass worth more.
2. **NMC car cells stand in for REON's sodium-ion stationary cells.** The spread multiplier and REON's field data are the way to close this.
3. **Extrapolation.** The data ends near 0.86 capacity; blocks retire at 0.70, so post-knee lines are extended with one correction (λ).
4. **The stationary timeline is assumed** (calendar and cycle fade, knee at 6.6 years). Quote percentages, not years.
5. **Simplified electrics.** Constant voltage, a count-based voltage window k, no thermal or UL limits.
6. **Static temperatures**, which make temperature resting effectively permanent.
7. **Economics are first-order**: no hourly dispatch optimization, demand-charge realization and peak duration are assumed, and ConnectedSolutions and Clean Peak are not stacked in the same hour. A ComStock load profile and PVWatts solar would replace the assumptions.
8. **Rates change**: ConnectedSolutions has no published program year in the materials found; Clean Peak rules changed in May 2026.
9. **Monte Carlo noise.** Python at 2,000 packs is the reference; the browser's 48-pack default reads about 1 point high.
10. **Sensing model.** Resting depends on how accurately the BMS measures each cell's capacity; the two implementations differ here by about $0.4k (Section 11). REON's sensing accuracy is not yet known.
11. **No SOC imbalance.** The paper finds it costs under 2% (βSOC above 0.98); REON's per-cell control could recover some of it, which is not counted.
12. **No public per-cell failure rate.** The fault results are shown at 10, 100 and 1,000 ppm per cell-year; which applies to REON's NFPP cells is a question for REON or its cell supplier.
13. **Economics leave out** interconnection, permitting, aggregator and software fees, and assume ConnectedSolutions pays $200/kW-summer for 20 years (rates are locked for 5).
